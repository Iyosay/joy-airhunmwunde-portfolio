SELECTED SYSTEMS / 2024 - 2026

I like architecture most when you can still explain the reasoning behind it six months later.

These are not meant as giant trophy cases. They are a few examples of how I think through ingestion, transformation, deployment, reliability, and operational tradeoffs.

01 / CLOUD ANALYTICS PLATFORM

Cocosurf Gear

Five daily nested JSON feeds, shaped into a cleaner analytical layer the team could actually trust.

AWS Snowflake dbt Airflow Docker ECS

The question

How do you keep semi-structured source data from becoming analytical chaos once it reaches production?

What I built

I worked on a pipeline that transformed nested JSON feeds from Amazon S3 into tested Snowflake models for customers, products, orders, order items, and inventory. The work was built with repeatability in mind, not just one successful run.

dbt was containerized, images were delivered through ECR, transformations ran on ECS Fargate, and orchestration tied the flow together. The result was a platform that turned raw source movement into something analysts could use more confidently.

02 / HISTORICAL MIGRATION

LuminaBrick Properties

Extract seven years of business-critical data without turning the live platform into a reporting engine.

PostgreSQL Python Validation Amazon S3

The constraint

The production platform needed to remain stable while a separate data science effort gained access to historical records.

The approach

I designed a schema-preserving extraction path across key PostgreSQL tables, with validation built into the handoff. The aim was to move data in a way that respected operational risk instead of pretending extraction was just a copy-and-paste exercise.

03 / RESEARCH INTO RELIABILITY

AI-Driven Schema Drift Detection

Trying to answer a practical question: when a schema changes, what breaks next and what should the platform do about it?

Apache Iceberg Python Graph modeling Random Forest

The idea

Schema drift is rarely just a local event. I explored a graph-based propagation model that could reason about downstream dependencies and pair that reasoning with ML-assisted severity classification.

Why it matters

The useful part was not the novelty for its own sake. It was the possibility of platforms responding with better judgment: log a minor issue, trigger review for a moderate one, quarantine risky data, or halt when trust is genuinely compromised.

FIELD NOTES / WORKING IDEAS / THINGS I'M TESTING

I keep a notebook for the parts of engineering that deserve slower thought.

01 · DATA RELIABILITY · 6 MIN

What schema drift actually breaks

A changed column is visible. The blast radius usually is not.

Schema drift often gets framed as a local quality issue: a field disappears, a type changes, a nested structure moves. But the harder engineering question is not just what changed. It is what depends on the thing that changed.

That dependency can reach transformations, tests, dashboards, ML features, and business reporting. So detection is not enough on its own. A useful platform also needs some way to reason about downstream impact.

02 · PLATFORM ENGINEERING · 5 MIN

Infrastructure as Code is a data engineering skill

Pipelines inherit the strengths and weaknesses of the environment around them.

Data discussions often end at SQL, Python, models, and orchestration. Production work does not. It depends on identity, storage, networking, compute, deployment, and permissions.

Infrastructure as Code makes those assumptions reviewable. It moves environment setup from tribal memory into versioned system design, which is healthier for teams and much kinder to future debugging.

03 · ANALYTICS ENGINEERING · 4 MIN

dbt is powerful. It is not the whole platform.

Transformation matters, but reliability lives across more layers than one tool can cover.

dbt gives structure to transformation work: modular models, tests, lineage, and documentation. That is valuable. But a production-grade data platform also lives in ingestion contracts, secrets handling, orchestration, deployment, monitoring, and failure recovery.

The clearer those boundaries are, the easier the whole system becomes to maintain.
